Comparison · SIBO vs IBS

SIBO vs IBS — one names your symptoms, the other explains them.

IBS is a diagnosis of exclusion: a label for bloating, pain, and altered bowel habits when tests find nothing. SIBO is a testable overgrowth that can produce those exact symptoms. For many people the two are not alternatives — the SIBO is sitting underneath the IBS label, untested.

The core difference

A description vs a mechanism.

IBS (irritable bowel syndrome) is defined by symptoms — abdominal pain plus altered bowel habits — once other diseases are ruled out. It is a diagnosis of exclusion: it tells you what you feel, not why. SIBO (small intestinal bacterial overgrowth) is a specific state — bacteria overgrowing where the small intestine should stay low in density — that is testable and produces the same symptoms. So they sit on different layers: IBS describes, SIBO explains.

The overlap

Much of what's called IBS tests positive for SIBO.

Studies that breath-test people diagnosed with IBS find SIBO in a large fraction — reported anywhere from roughly a third to the majority, depending on the test method and criteria. Because IBS is diagnosed by ruling other things out rather than by finding a cause, an untested overgrowth can live underneath the IBS label indefinitely. The tell is often the gas: a genuinely foul, sulfur smell points toward overgrowth (SRB) rather than a purely functional pattern.

Why it matters

The two labels point to opposite diets.

This is not a semantic argument — the treatments diverge. Standard IBS advice often means more fiber, probiotics, and fermented foods. If a SIBO is underneath, those are fuel: they can entrench the very bloating and gas they were meant to relieve. A SIBO-oriented approach goes the other way — lower the fermentation substrate and, where appropriate, reduce the overgrowth directly. Following the wrong map is why some "IBS" never improves.
