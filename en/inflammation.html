Mechanism · The two jobs of clearing

Clear the cause, and inflammation settles with it.

Long-standing pain, skin, and gut symptoms sometimes ease after an overgrowth is cleared. Killing microbes isn't the only reason — some of the agents used to clear it also press down on the body's inflammatory circuitry itself.

Check your symptom pattern · 60sStart with the SIBO guide
Q1

Why do other symptoms improve when you clear an overgrowth?

When symptoms that looked scattered (pain, skin, gut) in fact branch from one upstream source — bacterial or fungal overgrowth, reducing that source can quiet the downstream symptoms in the same direction. On top of this, some agents used to clear it (antibiotics, antifungals) lower the body's inflammatory response itself, independent of killing microbes. → When unrelated-looking symptoms improve together

Q2

The two jobs 'clearing' does at once

First, it lowers the load of overgrown bacteria and fungi (the antimicrobial job). Second, in the same process it presses down on the body's inflammatory circuitry — dialing back the switch that turns inflammation on and the enzymes that damage tissue. When both overlap, symptoms can settle faster and across a wider range.

① Lower the overgrowth

Reduce the bacterial/fungal load — the source of irritation itself shrinks.

② Quiet the inflammatory circuit

Dial back the inflammation switch, tissue-degrading enzymes, and inflammatory cells together.

Q3

What is the 'inflammatory circuitry'?

Two things, put simply. One is the master switch that turns inflammation on (technically NF-κB) — pressing it lowers the production of many inflammatory molecules together. The other is the enzyme that gnaws at and stiffens tissue once inflammation drags on (MMP) — blocking it reduces chronic damage and remodeling. Recruitment of inflammatory cells and reactive oxygen fall alongside.

Q4

Why symptoms that never showed on tests improve together

If symptoms that looked unrelated in fact branch from one overgrowth and its inflammatory circuitry, clearing that root can quiet several of them in the same direction. But this varies widely between people, and persisting symptoms must always be evaluated in person.

Q5

So can any drug be used to clear it?

No. The result depends on how you clear it. For the same clearing, some agents lower inflammation alongside, while others do not — or can even make symptoms temporarily worse early in treatment. The choice, dose, and duration must never be self-decided; they must be set through a clinical consultation.
