Symptom deep-dive · Brain Fog & Anxiety

Anxiety, brain fog, and fatigue may be "gas toxicity," not a psychological problem.

Chronic anxiety, mental fog, and fatigue that won't lift — with normal test results. When you re-inhale the sulfur gas you make yourself every day, the brain's energy factories (mitochondria) and its emotional center (the limbic system) are disturbed together.

1 · A toxic gas you re-inhale

Every day, you breathe in the gas you made.

Sulfur gas made in the gut circulates through breath and bloodstream. The problem is that this gas is toxic.

Energy-factory suppression

Mitochondrial toxicity

H₂S · CH₃SH

These gases carry cellular-respiration toxicity in the same class as cyanide. When cellular energy production is suppressed, it leads to chronic fatigue and brain fog.

Emotional-center damage

Limbic disturbance

re-inhaled

Re-inhaling the gas at low dose every day disturbs the limbic system that regulates emotion — anxiety, obsession, irritability, unreality.

Key

If tests say "nothing wrong" but anxiety, mental fog, and fatigue persist, it is worth suspecting not only a psychological cause but the gas you produce.

3 · The gut-brain axis is not a metaphor

A chemical made in the gut touches the brain directly.

The "gut-brain axis" is not an abstract figure of speech — it is a real route by which a measurable gas reaches the brain and emotional centers. That is why digestive symptoms and mental symptoms so often come together.
