When the skin keeps flaring, no matter what you put on it

Not one patient's chart, but a picture I meet often — anonymized and composited. When creams and cleansers clear it and it comes right back, I stop treating the surface and follow the line of reasoning to where it keeps returning from.

The gut–skin axisRead the essays
01 · The pattern

The picture I keep seeing

Acne, folliculitis, seborrheic dermatitis, rosacea — treated at the surface with washes, creams, sometimes courses of antibiotics. It clears, then comes right back. And often, quietly, there are gut symptoms alongside it: bloating, irregular stools, a sensitive stomach.

02 · The usual name

What it usually gets called

"A skin condition," "hormonal," "sensitive skin." Each name keeps the whole problem on the face — and treats it there, again and again.

03 · Where I stop and doubt

Here is where I pause and doubt

If the skin has been treated properly and it still keeps returning, the honest thought isn't "a stronger cream." It's: maybe this was never only a skin problem.

Doubt

Skin that flares back on a schedule, alongside gut symptoms, is a signal to look below the skin — not just harder at it.

04 · Following the thread

Following the thread

1. The gut and skin talk constantly — the gut–skin axis. Metabolites and gases from the gut reach the skin's environment.

2. When there's overgrowth upstream, that traffic shifts: sebum, inflammation, and the balance of skin organisms (Malassezia, Staphylococcus) move with it.

3. So the flare that reads as "facial" often tracks the gut — worse when the gut is worse, quieter when it settles.

Key

When skin keeps returning after proper topical care, look for the driver upstream in the gut — the face is often reporting, not originating.

05 · One root, two organisms

Bacteria or fungus — the same root

Trace it upstream and it arrives where the others do: overgrowth in the small bowel — bacterial (SIBO / VSC-producing bacteria) or fungal (SIFO / Candida, mirrored on the skin by Malassezia). Either way, the logic holds: too much fermentation, too high up.

Note

Treat only the face and the upstream keeps running — which is why it keeps coming back. The useful question is "what feeds it, and where," not "which cream."

06 · The direction I'd weigh

So which way I'd lean

If the driver is upstream, the logical move is to aim at the fuel and the site of fermentation rather than adding one more thing at the surface. What exactly, and how much, varies from person to person — that part belongs in consultation. This note shows the logic of the approach, not a prescription.

07 · Limits

What this note is not
