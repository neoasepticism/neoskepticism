About

June-sang Yang, MD

A physician in Gangnam, Seoul who, for eight years, has measured the gas rather than guessed at it — reading chronic symptoms as downstream of bacterial overgrowth in the gut.

The work

Measuring what others estimate.

For eight years at a clinic in Gangnam, Seoul, June-sang Yang, MD has directly measured patients' breath gases — including hydrogen sulfide (H₂S) and methyl mercaptan (CH₃SH), the sulfur gases that standard breath panels routinely leave out. That measurement is the backbone of the clinical frame he calls Neo-Skepticism: many chronic, hard-to-pin-down symptoms — bloating, bad breath, brain fog, cravings, skin problems — are downstream effects of bacterial overgrowth in the gut, not separate diseases.

The recurring, counterintuitive observation is that the foods widely taught as "healthy" — fiber, whole grains, sulfur-rich vegetables, fermented foods, probiotics — can act as fuel for that overgrowth. The frame is not a single prescriptive diet; it is a way of reading the body's signals, with the smell and composition of the gas as a diagnostic thread.

Background

Translator, clinician, writer.

Alongside clinical practice, he is the Korean translator of low-carbohydrate / high-fat books including The Big Fat Surprise (Nina Teicholz) — work that shaped his skepticism toward received nutritional consensus. He writes an English-language essay series on Substack and posts as @neoskepticMD, with a Korean-language clinical blog at blog.naver.com/healthcrew.
